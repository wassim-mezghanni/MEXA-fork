\chapter{\abstractname}

Standard multilingual benchmarks often conflate internal representation quality with prompt sensitivity, task-specific reasoning, and decoding strategies. Under the pivot hypothesis, multilingual transformers develop a shared concept space in their intermediate layers, mapping semantically equivalent inputs to nearby representations. This thesis evaluates internal cross-lingual representation alignment using MEXA, a training-free framework that extracts layer-wise hidden states and measures bidirectional retrieval accuracy on parallel sentences.

We evaluate 16 transformer architectures across four distinct paradigms: dense causal decoders, sparse Mixture-of-Experts, dedicated contrastive sentence encoders, and masked language models, covering FLORES-200 and the 1,401-language Super-Parallel Bible Corpus. Parameter scaling and multilingual pre-training substantially improve internal alignment, while relative language difficulty remains highly consistent across modern and archaic domains ($\rho = 0.98$). In sparse MoE models, routing decisions reflect syntactic and semantic functions rather than language identity, preserving the shared concept space. Furthermore, testing non-English pivots (French, German, Arabic, Chinese, Basque) shows that English is not an obligatory structural hub; subtracting language-specific centroid offsets aligns representations across all pivots above 94\% retrieval accuracy. Finally, internal alignment scores strongly predict downstream zero-shot accuracy on Belebele reading comprehension and m-ARC reasoning ($\rho = 1.00$). Together, these results demonstrate that training-free representation alignment provides a reliable, low-cost diagnostic for understanding multilingual foundation models.
