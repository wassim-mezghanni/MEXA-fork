\chapter{\abstractname}

Multilingual language models process text across diverse languages, raising the fundamental question of how they represent concepts across linguistic boundaries. However, standard multilingual benchmarks often conflate internal representation quality with prompt sensitivity, task-specific reasoning, and decoding strategies. Under the pivot hypothesis, multilingual transformers develop a shared concept space in their intermediate layers, mapping semantically equivalent inputs to nearby representations regardless of surface language. This thesis evaluates internal cross-lingual representation alignment using MEXA \citep{kargaran2025mexa}, a training-free probing framework that extracts and pools hidden states across model layers. Alignment is quantified through bidirectional sentence retrieval using English as the primary pivot language, measuring how reliably a sentence in a target language and its English translation mutually identify each other as closest nearest neighbors among a pool of competing distractor sentences.

We evaluate 16 transformer architectures across four distinct model categories: dense causal decoders, sparse Mixture-of-Experts, dedicated contrastive sentence encoders, and masked language models. Evaluations span two parallel corpora: FLORES-200 \citep{nllbteam2022} across 204 modern language varieties, and the Super-Parallel Bible Corpus \citep{mayer2014bible} across 1,401 typologically diverse languages down to the low-resource tail. Parameter scaling and multilingual pre-training improve internal alignment, while relative language difficulty remains consistent across modern and archaic domains. In sparse MoE models, routing decisions reflect syntactic and semantic functions rather than language identity, preserving the shared concept space. Furthermore, we test alignment across five non-English pivot languages: French, German, Arabic, Chinese, and Basque. These experiments show that English is not an obligatory structural hub, as subtracting language-specific centroid offsets aligns representations across all pivots near ceiling accuracy. Finally, internal alignment scores strongly predict downstream zero-shot accuracy across two multilingual benchmarks: Belebele reading comprehension \citep{bandarkar2024belebele} and m-ARC reasoning \citep{lai2023okapi}. These findings show that training-free representation alignment serves as an efficient diagnostic for how multilingual models form shared concept spaces and transfer knowledge across languages.
