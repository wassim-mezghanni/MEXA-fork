\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

Building upon the MEXA framework, this thesis investigated how multilingual models structure and share representations across languages in their intermediate layers. We extended the scope of training-free alignment evaluation across 27 publicly available checkpoints representing 16 model architectures across four architectural categories: dense causal decoders, sparse Mixture-of-Experts, masked language models, and dedicated multilingual sentence encoders. Using two multi-way parallel corpora covering up to 1,400 languages, this work examined parameter scaling, sparse routing dynamics in Mixture-of-Experts, representation geometry and tokenization effects.

\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}

The main findings and empirical contributions of this thesis are:

\paragraph{Parameter scaling and domain robustness.}
Across new open-weight decoder families (including Qwen~3, Qwen~3.5, and Apertus), cross-lingual alignment scales consistently with model capacity. Within the Qwen~3 family, scaling from 0.6B to 8B parameters yields steady gains in peak alignment on FLORES-200 ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.35 \rightarrow 0.57$). Furthermore, relative model performance rankings remain highly consistent across distinct domains ($\rho = 0.98$ across models evaluated on the 116 Belebele-overlapping FLORES pairs and 101-language Bible subsets), showing that internal alignment reflects underlying linguistic competence rather than domain-specific memorization. Similarly, expanding sample size to the full 1,012 FLORES sentences and evaluating the complete 204 language-script pairs and 1,401 Bible pairs decreases absolute scores due to higher distractor density, but leaves relative model rankings unchanged ($\rho \ge 0.99$).

\paragraph{Architectural paradigms and layerwise trajectories.}
Comparing causal decoders to dedicated multilingual sentence encoders and masked language models revealed distinct representational trajectories across transformer layer depth. Dedicated multilingual sentence encoders (LaBSE, mE5) achieve high alignment early in the network ($\mu_{\text{Max}} > 0.85$ by Layer 6 - 8), likely due to their explicit contrastive training objectives. In contrast, causal decoders construct a shared concept space progressively, reaching peak alignment in upper-middle layers (at a relative layer depth of 60\% to 80\%) before diverging at final output heads to accommodate language-specific vocabulary projection.

\paragraph{Mixture-of-Experts and routing dynamics.}
Sparse Mixture-of-Experts models (Mixtral~8x7B, Mixtral~8x22B, and Gemma~4~26B-A4B) match or exceed dense baselines with comparable per-token active compute. Analysis of token-level gating showed that routing decisions are driven by token function and context rather than language membership, meaning experts do not segregate into language-specific silos. In intermediate layers, gating distributions converge to balanced, near-uniform utilization across all 8 experts ($\approx 12.5\%$ each) across diverse language families, confirming that sparse conditional execution preserves the shared concept space just as effectively as dense architectures.

\paragraph{Pivot invariance and representation geometry.}
Evaluating alignment across six typologically diverse pivot languages (English, French, German, Arabic, Chinese, and Basque) shows that internal representations are not inherently English-centric. High-resource non-English pivots match or exceed English alignment under standard retrieval. Once language-specific centroid offsets are removed by mean-centering, alignment across all six pivots converges to a near-identical ceiling ($\mu_{\text{Max}} \approx 0.94$ - $0.96$ for Qwen~3.5~9B). This confirms that intermediate layers encode a language-agnostic concept space, where semantic meaning determines representation position rather than surface language.

\paragraph{The tokenizer fertility tax.}
Subword tokenization heavily penalizes low-resource and non-Latin scripts across the 204 language-script pairs in FLORES-200. Tokenizer fertility shows a strong negative relationship with alignment scores, a pattern that persists within individual script families including Cyrillic, Arabic, and Latin scripts. Highly fragmented subword sequences dilute positional pooling weights and introduce noise into sentence representations, creating an artificial performance barrier independent of the model's underlying representational capacity.

\paragraph{Downstream predictive validity.}
Validating MEXA alignment against downstream task benchmarks confirmed that internal representation sharing directly predicts practical multilingual capability. At the model level, higher MEXA scores consistently predict stronger zero-shot accuracy on both Belebele reading comprehension and m-ARC scientific reasoning evaluated independently. At the individual language level, per-language MEXA alignment in Llama~3.1~8B strongly predicts per-language comprehension accuracy across 110 languages, showing that internal geometry directly underpins downstream task performance.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion-limitations}

While the findings offer consistent insights into LLM representation geometry, several limitations should be noted:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parallel corpus dependencies and domain shifts.} Evaluation relies on sentence-level translation quality. While FLORES-200 provides professionally translated modern prose, the massively multilingual Bible corpus contains archaic phrasing, stylized syntax, and religious motifs that do not fully reflect contemporary vernacular language usage.
    
    \item \textbf{Tokenization bias in representation pooling.} MEXA computes sentence representations via token-level pooling over model hidden states. Consequently, high subword fertility in low-resource scripts mechanically dilutes token weights under positional pooling, conflating subword vocabulary fragmentation with intrinsic semantic representation quality.
    
    \item \textbf{Computational cost of layerwise embedding extraction.} Extracting and storing full hidden state tensors across all transformer layers for large models (e.g., 70B parameters) over thousands of sentences requires substantial GPU memory and disk storage. Consequently, evaluation in this work is restricted to static released checkpoints rather than tracking alignment dynamics at frequent intervals throughout the pre-training process.
    
    \item \textbf{Absence of open-ended generation dynamics.} While MEXA correlates strongly with zero-shot classification and multiple-choice reasoning benchmarks (such as Belebele and m-ARC), it does not directly evaluate autoregressive generative behavior, hallucination tendencies, or safety alignment during free-form text generation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future-work}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vocabulary adaptation and fertility mitigation.} Developing dynamic tokenizer expansion techniques or fertility-normalized pooling functions to decouple subword fragmentation penalties from semantic alignment evaluation in low-resource scripts.
    
    \item \textbf{Alignment-guided MoE pruning and routing.} Utilizing layerwise alignment trajectories as an objective to guide expert pruning, router regularization, and expert allocation for multilingual adaptation in sparse models.
    
    \item \textbf{Causal representation steering.} Applying activation patching and steering vectors \citep{meng2022locating, zou2023representation, wendler2024llamas} to intermediate representation layers to investigate whether boosting alignment directly enhances downstream cross-lingual reasoning transfer in low-resource, high-fertility languages (such as Amharic or Burmese) that exhibit lower baseline alignment.
    
    \item \textbf{Multimodal and speech representation alignment.} Extending the geometric alignment and mean-centering framework of MEXA to vision-language and speech-to-text foundation models, evaluating cross-modal representation convergence across diverse languages.
\end{enumerate}
