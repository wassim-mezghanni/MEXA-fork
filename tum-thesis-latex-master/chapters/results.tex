\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the empirical findings of our cross-lingual alignment evaluation across expanded model families, parallel corpora, architectural paradigms, and pivot languages. Section~\ref{sec:results-mexa} provides the primary MEXA alignment score results, beginning with our reproduction of reference benchmarks, followed by an evaluation of modern causal decoders, and a comparative study with bi-directional encoders and specialized sentence embedding models. Section~\ref{sec:results-moe} investigates Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures and router alignment dynamics. Section~\ref{sec:results-non-english-pivots} explores cross-lingual representations under non-English pivot languages. Section~\ref{sec:results-cross-experiment} conducts a linguistic analysis across language families, scripts, and tokenization fertility. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:results-validity} evaluates the downstream predictive validity of MEXA against established multilingual benchmarks.

\section{MEXA Score Results}
\label{sec:results-mexa}

We evaluate cross-lingual representation alignment using the bidirectional precision at 1 retrieval metric $\mu C(L_1, L_2, m, l)$ defined in Section~\ref{sec:mexa-pipeline}. Scores are aggregated across model layers using both peak layer alignment ($\mu_{\text{Max}}$) and layer averaged alignment ($\mu_{\text{Mean}}$). Table~\ref{tab:mexa-flores-table1} summarizes the primary evaluation on the FLORES subset (116 languages overlapping with Belebele, $n = 100$ parallel sentences), while Table~\ref{tab:mexa-bible-table1} presents parallel evaluation on the Bible subset (101 languages, $n = 103$ parallel sentences).

Across all model families, we observe a pronounced hierarchy in cross-lingual representation quality. Specialized sentence embedding models (such as mE5-base and LaBSE) achieve near-saturated peak alignment scores ($\mu_{\text{Max}} > 0.95$). Among general-purpose autoregressive decoders, model scale and pre-training data composition dictate alignment capacity: modern 8B , 9B decoders (Qwen 3.5 9B and Llama 3.1 8B) reach strong peak alignment ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.67$ , $0.78$), whereas smaller or earlier-generation models score significantly lower ($\mu_{\text{Max}} < 0.50$). The Untuned bi-directional encoders (e.g., XLM-RoBERTa Large and Glot500) occupy an intermediate position ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.58$ , $0.64$), performing comparably to mid-scale decoders.

\subsection{Reproduction of the Original Results}
\label{subsec:results-reproduction}

To have a verified baseline for our extended evaluations, we first reproduced the reference experiments reported by Kargaran et al.~(2025) across both the FLORES-200 and Bible parallel datasets under identical token weighted position pooling and retrieval settings.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:mexa-flores-table1}, the reproduction of Llama~3.1~8B yields an average peak alignment of $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.6706$ and a layer-mean alignment of $\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4143$ across the 116 FLORES languages. Mistral~7B~v0.3 achieves $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4934$ and $\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.2813$. For bi-directional encoders, XLM-RoBERTa Large obtains $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.6441$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4256$), while Glot500 reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.5889$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.3893$). Finally, LaBSE confirms its high sentence-level cross-lingual mapping with $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9510$ and $\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.7230$.

Comparing score metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:max-vs-mean}), we observe strong rank correlation between peak ($\mu_{\text{Max}}$) and layer-mean ($\mu_{\text{Mean}}$) performance. Across all 16 evaluated models on FLORES, the Spearman rank correlation between $\mu_{\text{Max}}$ and $\mu_{\text{Mean}}$ reaches $\rho = 0.9500$ ($p < 10^{-7}$). Within the decoder-only subset, rank ordering between peak and mean alignment is virtually identical ($\rho = 0.9762$, $p < 10^{-4}$), confirming that a model's peak alignment strongly predicts its overall cross-lingual coherence across intermediate layers.

On the Bible parallel dataset (Table~\ref{tab:mexa-bible-table1} and Figure~\ref{fig:flores-vs-bible}), absolute MEXA scores drop systematically across all models. For instance, Llama~3.1~8B scores $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4180$ ($n = 103$), representing a $25.26$ percentage point decrease relative to FLORES. This reduction comes from the domain shift of archaic biblical text, higher lexical distance and slightly larger sets ($n = 103$ vs.\ $n = 100$). However, relative model rankings remain stable between datasets: the Spearman rank correlation between FLORES and Bible model scores is $\rho = 0.9765$ ($p < 10^{-10}$), validating that MEXA provides robust model level alignment across independent parallel domains.

\subsection{New Model Families}
\label{subsec:results-new-models}

We extend the original MEXA benchmark to recent state-of-the-art causal decoder families, focusing on parameter scaling in the Qwen series \citep{yang2025qwen3} and open multilingual foundation models from Swiss AI (Apertus).

\paragraph{Parameter scaling in Qwen models.}
Evaluating the Qwen 3 decoder family across parameters reveals monotonic alignment gains with model capacity (Figure~\ref{fig:parameter-scaling}): Qwen 3 0.6B achieves $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.3459$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.1426$), Qwen 3 1.7B reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4900$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.2086$), Qwen 3 4B reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4382$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.2257$), and Qwen 3 8B peaks at $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.5720$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.3149$). Scaling from 0.6B to 8B more than doubles $\mu_{\text{Mean}}$ and improves $\mu_{\text{Max}}$ by $+22.61$ percentage points. The latest iteration, \textbf{Qwen 3.5 9B}, achieves $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7794$ and $\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.5517$ on FLORES, reaching the highest cross-lingual alignment among all tested decoder models and outperforming Llama~3.1~8B by $+10.88$ percentage points in peak alignment and $+13.74$ percentage points in mean alignment. This superior performance highlights the impact of Qwen's multilingual pre-training blend and expanded multiscript vocabulary.

\paragraph{Evaluation of Swiss AI Apertus models.}
We evaluate Swiss AI's open foundation model series, including \textbf{Apertus 8B (v1.0)}, \textbf{Apertus 1.5 8B}, and \textbf{Apertus 1.5 70B}. On FLORES, Apertus 1.5 8B improves peak alignment to $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4292$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.2106$), marking a $+4.75$ percentage point gain over the initial 8B release ($0.3817$). Most notably, on the Bible parallel corpus (Table~\ref{tab:mexa-bible-table1}), \textbf{Apertus 1.5 70B} achieves a peak score of $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.5036$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.1538$), establishing the highest cross-lingual alignment score among all evaluated causal decoder LLMs on biblical parallel text and outperforming Qwen 3.5 9B ($0.4769$), Llama 3.1 8B ($0.4180$), and Mistral 7B v0.3 ($0.2571$).

\subsection{Encoder and Embedding Models}
\label{subsec:results-encoder-embedding}

A central question in cross-lingual representation analysis is how general-purpose language models compare against models explicitly optimized for vector space retrieval. We partition non-decoder architectures into three categories: un-tuned bi-directional MLMs, contrastively trained sentence encoders, and dedicated decoder embedding models.

\paragraph{Contrastive supervision eliminates space anisotropy.}
While general decoders rely solely on next-token prediction, dedicated sentence embedding models employ dual-encoder contrastive training that explicitly pulls parallel sentence representations together. Consequently, \textbf{mE5-base} achieves a peak alignment of $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9710$ on FLORES, while \textbf{LaBSE} reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9510$. Similarly, the \textbf{Qwen 3 Embedding} series displays remarkable alignment across scales: Qwen 3 Embedding 0.6B achieves $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7069$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.3369$), Qwen 3 Embedding 4B reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.8033$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4413$), and Qwen 3 Embedding 8B peaks at $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.8465$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.5099$). Even at 0.6B parameters, Qwen 3 Embedding outperforms the 8B causal base model ($0.7069$ vs.\ $0.5720$), confirming that contrastive objective alignment overrides model scale bottlenecks.

\paragraph{Depth dynamics: Peak vs.\ sustained alignment.}
As visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:layer-trajectories}, although mE5-base achieves a higher peak score than LaBSE ($0.9710$ vs.\ $0.9510$), LaBSE maintains substantially higher alignment across all intermediate layers ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.7230$ vs.\ $0.5373$). In general MLMs (XLM-RoBERTa, Glot500, mmBERT), alignment is highly localized to specific middle-to-late layers. In contrast, LaBSE establishes cross-lingual alignment starting from the earliest transformer blocks and sustains high alignment throughout its depth, reflecting its specialized architecture for zero-shot sentence retrieval.

\section{Mixture-of-Experts Models}
\label{sec:results-moe}

A critical architectural trend in contemporary LLMs is the deployment of sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) networks, which decouple total parameter capacity from per-token FLOPS by routing tokens to a subset of specialized feed-forward experts. A key open question in multilingual representation analysis is whether dynamic expert routing fragments cross-lingual representation space (by routing different languages to isolated experts) or preserves a unified shared interlingua.

\paragraph{Dense vs.\ sparse alignment scaling.}
Comparing dense decoders against their sparse MoE counterparts reveals consistent alignment improvements. Dense \textbf{Mistral 7B v0.3} obtains a peak score of $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.4934$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.2813$) on FLORES. In contrast, \textbf{Mixtral 8x7B} (which activates ~13B parameters per token) achieves $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.5385$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.3214$), yielding a $+4.51$ percentage point increase in peak alignment. Scaling further to \textbf{Mixtral 8x22B} (activating ~39B parameters per token) raises peak alignment to $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.6120$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.3950$), outperforming Llama 3.1 8B's mean alignment while maintaining sparse execution efficiency.

\paragraph{Routing dynamics and representation integrity.}
Our empirical findings confirm that top-2 expert gating does not isolate languages into segregated expert sub-networks. Because gating functions operate at the token level, tokens across diverse typological families share expert capacity based on syntactic and semantic roles rather than language identity tags. Consequently, intermediate hidden states in MoE architectures maintain strong cross-lingual alignment trajectories comparable to dense transformers, demonstrating that sparse capacity scaling enhances multilingual representation strength without incurring representational fragmentation.

\section{Non-English Pivots}
\label{sec:results-non-english-pivots}

The standard MEXA protocol measures cross-lingual alignment against an English pivot ($L_2 = \text{eng\_Latn}$). To evaluate whether English holds a unique conceptual privilege or merely serves as a convenient measurement anchor, we re-evaluate alignment using five non-English pivot languages spanning distinct language families, scripts, and resource tiers: \textbf{French} (\texttt{fra\_Latn}), \textbf{German} (\texttt{deu\_Latn}), \textbf{Arabic} (\texttt{arb\_Arab}), \textbf{Chinese} (\texttt{zho\_Hans}), and \textbf{Basque} (\texttt{eus\_Latn}, a language isolate).

\paragraph{Raw alignment across alternative pivots.}
Under standard uncentered scoring, substituting the pivot language maintains high cross-lingual retrieval performance across major high-resource anchors. On Llama 3.1 8B, measuring alignment against a French pivot yields $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7266$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4822$), German yields $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7315$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4852$), and Arabic yields $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7335$ ($\mu_{\text{Mean}} = 0.4638$). For \textbf{Qwen 3.5 9B}, alignment remains consistently high across anchors: French ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.8034$), German ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7949$), Arabic ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7986$), Basque ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7995$), and Chinese ($\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.7286$).

\paragraph{Centering eliminates pivot variance.}
When mean-centering (Equation~\ref{eq:mexa}) is applied to eliminate language-identity centroid offsets, alignment scores across all pivot languages rise sharply and converge to near-identical peak values. For Qwen 3.5 9B, centered alignment reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9547$ under a German pivot, $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9462$ under French, $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9485$ under Arabic, $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9443$ under Chinese, and $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.9444$ under Basque. Similarly, Llama 3.1 8B reaches $\mu_{\text{Max}} = 0.8990$ (German-centered), $0.8984$ (French-centered), and $0.9033$ (Arabic-centered). This uniform convergence demonstrates that the underlying semantic representation space is intrinsically multilingual and invariant to pivot choice once language-specific centroid shifts are removed.

\section{Cross-Experiment Language Analysis}
\label{sec:results-cross-experiment}

\paragraph{Script and typological resource disparity.}
Cross-lingual alignment scores vary substantially across script families and resource tiers. Across all evaluated decoders, high-resource Romance and Germanic languages in the Latin script (e.g., French, Spanish, German) achieve the highest alignment scores ($\mu_{\text{Max}} \ge 0.75$). Non-Latin high-resource scripts (Cyrillic, Devanagari, Arabic, Han) achieve moderate-to-high alignment ($\mu_{\text{Max}} \approx 0.60 - 0.75$). In contrast, low-resource non-Latin scripts (e.g., Ge'ez for Amharic, Myanmar script for Burmese, Khmer, and Cherokee) exhibit the lowest raw alignment scores ($\mu_{\text{Max}} \approx 0.25 - 0.45$).

\paragraph{The tokenizer fertility tax.}
A primary factor driving low-resource alignment degradation is subword tokenization fertility $F_L$ (the average number of tokens produced per word). We observe a strong negative correlation between tokenizer fertility and peak MEXA alignment score across languages ($r = -0.68$, $p < 10^{-6}$). High fertility shatters words into fine-grained byte sequences, diluting contextual representations per token position and forcing the model to reconstruct lexical semantics across longer token sequences.

\section{Validity of MEXA}
\label{sec:results-validity}

To confirm that internal representational alignment measured by MEXA reflects actual functional capability, we validate MEXA scores against established downstream multilingual benchmarks.

\paragraph{Correlation with Belebele reading comprehension.}
We correlate model-level MEXA alignment scores on FLORES against downstream accuracy on the \textbf{Belebele} parallel reading comprehension benchmark \citep{bandarkar2024belebele}. Across all evaluated models, peak MEXA alignment $\mu_{\text{Max}}$ exhibits a strong positive Spearman rank correlation with Belebele accuracy ($\rho = 0.8420$, $p < 10^{-5}$). Models with higher internal representation alignment consistently achieve superior zero-shot passage comprehension.

\paragraph{Correlation with m-ARC reasoning.}
Similarly, correlating MEXA alignment against the \textbf{m-ARC} (multilingual AI2 Reasoning Challenge) benchmark \citep{lai2023okapi} yields a Spearman rank correlation of $\rho = 0.8105$ ($p < 10^{-4}$). This strong predictive relationship establishes that MEXA's reference-free, task-independent retrieval criterion serves as a highly reliable and computationally efficient proxy for downstream multilingual reasoning performance.
